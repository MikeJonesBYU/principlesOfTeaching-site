The CS 356 "User Study Rubric" (Canvas, course 36062, rubric 198046 — finalized 2026-08-19) grades every user-study assignment. Mike plans to use it to generate case-study examples at different performance levels (full-marks / developing / beginning), replacing the earlier annotated-narrative approach in `examples/`. Weighting deliberately escalates from procedure to judgment: Methods 15 → Results & Analysis 20 → Design Decisions 25 (42% of the grade is converting findings into design commitments).

## Methods (15 pts)
- **Full Marks (15):** Recruited the required number of participants (no extra credit for more). Followed the standard methods for this kind of study, with at most a minor error or two and no major ones.
- **Developing (10):** Recruited more than half of the required participants, but not all. Methods mostly align with the standard for this study type; several minor errors, possibly one significant one.
- **Beginning (5):** Recruited half or fewer of the required participants. Methods are sloppy and don't align with the standard methods for this kind of study; the protocol contains significant errors.
- **No Marks (0):** No study conducted, or the work submitted does not resemble the assigned study type.

## Results and Analysis (20 pts)
Criterion note: raw data (notes, matrices, task tables) must be included as an appendix.
- **Full Marks (20):** Results are clear and concise (tables, counts, representative quotes). Analysis synthesizes across participants — patterns, agreements, disagreements — not session-by-session recaps. Claims are calibrated to what the data supports. Raw data included as an appendix.
- **Developing (13):** Results accurate but presented participant-by-participant with little synthesis, OR the analysis over-claims (one comment treated as a universal finding, conclusions beyond the data). **Over-claiming caps a submission at this level regardless of polish.** Raw data incomplete or hard to connect to the analysis.
- **Beginning (7):** Results disorganized, incomplete, or bury the findings. Little or no real analysis — data without interpretation, or speculation disconnected from what participants did and said. Raw data missing.
- **No Marks (0):** No results presented.

## Design Decisions (25 pts)
Criterion note: every decision should cite the finding that motivates it. No extra credit for volume — five well-cited decisions beat fifteen loose ones.
- **Full Marks (25):** Every decision explicitly cites its finding ("Because 6 of 8 sorters grouped X with Y, we will…"). Most decisions concrete enough to act on; a few directional ones are fine where data doesn't support a specific fix. Evidence-backed decisions to KEEP something unchanged count fully.
- **Developing (17):** Decisions reasonable and consistent with findings, but connections implicit — reader must infer which result motivated which decision. Some decisions have no visible tie to the data, or are too vague to commit to anything ("improve the labels").
- **Beginning (8):** Either failure or both: decisions ignore/contradict the data (a plan made before the study ran — the study was theater), or no real decisions, just restated findings with no commitments.
- **No Marks (0):** No design decisions presented.

**Design intent (from Mike's answers):** high R&A = cross-participant synthesis; over-claiming is a significant error; raw-data appendix required. High DD = explicit citation per decision; mix of concrete + directional decisions OK; "keep" decisions count fully; low = untethered decisions OR no decisions. The three criteria form a chain of custody: trustworthy data → calibrated findings → cited decisions.
