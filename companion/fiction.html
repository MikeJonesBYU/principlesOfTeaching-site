CS 356 Companion · Honesty statement

What’s real and what’s not

This companion mixes a real design project with fictitious user research, on purpose. That is a dangerous thing to do carelessly, so this page states exactly where the line falls. If anything elsewhere in the companion seems to blur it, this page wins.

What is real

  • The site.

    The Principles of Teaching site was really designed, really built, and is really deployed. It is used by real teachers. It is not a class exercise and was not made up for this companion.

  • The design decisions.

    Every decision a study lands on — the problem-driven entry point, the seven problem categories in teacher language, keeping the browse-by-principle path alongside it, forgiving search, three visually identical home buttons — is a decision that was actually made and actually shipped.

  • The git history, and the commits the studies cite.

    Decisions are cited to the commit where they landed (782ddb5, 52f8dda, e2bf223, d476c8c, 0dcf6c5, and the de-fidelity experiment on the sketched branch, 20bae25). Those hashes are checkable. The archived versions in this companion, as each one is built, are exported directly from those commits.

  • The methods.

    The contextual study, card sort, tree test, five-user task test, and external review are described as they are actually taught and actually run. The study designs are meant to be copied.

What is invented

FICTITIOUS DATA

Nobody ran these studies. There were no sessions, no recordings, no recruiting, no consent forms, no analysis of anyone’s real behavior.

Everything below was written for this companion:

  • The ward.

    The Timpanogos Shadows Ward does not exist. It is an invented Utah County unit created so the studies have a consistent setting.

  • Every participant.

    Every named person who appears as a participant or reviewer is fictitious. Their callings, ages, phones, and personalities are invented, and are kept consistent from study to study so the arc reads as one project rather than six unrelated ones. They are not based on real individuals.

  • Every quote.

    No one said any of it. Quotes are written to sound like something a teacher might plausibly say, which is exactly what makes them dangerous if they were ever mistaken for evidence.

  • Every number.

    Task success rates, times, similarity matrices, dendrograms, counts of users who did anything — all invented. They are constructed to be internally plausible for the method, not measured.

Why the fiction is here at all

CS 356 needs a worked example that runs end to end: a brief, a real artifact, a sequence of methods, and decisions that visibly follow from findings. Real projects rarely supply all of that in a form a class can read in a semester — the research that shaped this site happened in conversations, in teaching experience, and in the designer’s own head, not in a tidy set of documented sessions.

So the companion reconstructs it. The fiction dramatizes a real design rationale; it does not fabricate one. Each invented study was written backwards from a decision that was genuinely made, and is constructed to be the study that would have produced it. That direction of construction is the whole trick, and it is also the honest limit of the exercise.

The honest limit

Invented data can never surprise you. Real participants routinely destroy the hypothesis you brought them, and that is the single most valuable thing user research does. Nothing in this companion can demonstrate that, because the findings were written by the person who already knew the answer. Read these studies as models of form — how a study is designed, run, reported, and turned into a decision — and get your surprises from real users.

How invented material is marked

The rule is absolute: invented data is always visibly marked, at the point where it appears. Four layers do this.

  • 1. The banner at the top of every page.

    Injected by companion.js on every companion page, with no way to opt a page out. It is the red bar you scrolled past to get here.

  • 2. The FICTITIOUS DATA stamp.

    Attached directly to every invented table, chart, quote block, and number — so that a screenshot, a printout, or a single scrolled section still carries the warning with it.

  • 3. A scope note at the top of every study page.

    Naming the specifics for that page: the artifact and the decision are real; every participant, session, quote, and number between them is invented.

  • 4. This page.

    The canonical statement, linked from the banner on every page of the companion.

If you are ever unsure

Assume any person, session, quote, or number in this companion is invented, and any page, commit, or design decision on the live site is real. That heuristic is correct everywhere in the arc.
