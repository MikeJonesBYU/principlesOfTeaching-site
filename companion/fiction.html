What’s real and what’s not

This companion mixes a real design project with fictitious user research, on purpose — and it goes one step further than most design fiction: the invented studies drive a real redesign that real people will use. That is a dangerous thing to do carelessly, so this page states exactly where the line falls and what rules keep it honest. If anything elsewhere in the companion seems to blur it, this page wins.

What is real

  • The site.

    The Principles of Teaching site was really designed, really built, and is really deployed. It is used by real teachers. In this companion it plays the role Project 1 assigns to “an existing version you’re unimpressed by” — the starting point of the redesign.

  • The prototypes.

    The wireframe, the first functional prototype, and the final prototype are real working artifacts. They were actually built, you can actually click through them, and once a study has been run against one, it is frozen so the study’s citations stay checkable. The final prototype will really be deployed as the next version of the site.

  • The card deck.

    The 53 cards in the card sort are the site’s 53 skills, verbatim from the Church’s Teacher Development Skills manual. Study instruments built from real content carry a CONSTRUCTED badge with a traceability key, rather than being stamped fictitious — stamping real content “fictitious” would itself be a small lie.

  • The design decisions — once shipped.

    Every decision a study page lands on is really implemented in the next prototype in the chain, and the git history records it. The decisions are real commitments, checkable in the artifacts and the commits.

  • The methods.

    The open card sort, the tree test, and the five-user task test are described as they are actually taught and actually run. The study designs are meant to be copied.

What is invented

FICTITIOUS DATA

Nobody ran these studies. There were no sessions, no recordings, no recruiting, no consent forms, no analysis of anyone’s real behavior.

Everything below was written for this companion:

  • The ward.

    The Timpanogos Shadows Ward does not exist. It is an invented Utah County unit created so the studies have a consistent setting.

  • Every participant.

    Every named person who appears as a participant is fictitious. Their callings, ages, phones, and personalities are invented, and are kept consistent from study to study so the chain reads as one project. They are not based on real individuals.

  • Every quote.

    No one said any of it. Quotes are written to sound like something a teacher might plausibly say, which is exactly what makes them dangerous if they were ever mistaken for evidence.

  • Every number.

    Sort agreements, task success rates, times, paths, counts of users who did anything — all invented. They are constructed to be internally plausible for the method, not measured.

The part that needs extra care: the fiction runs forward

Most design fiction dramatizes decisions that were already made. This companion does something stronger: the invented studies come first, and the real redesign follows them. The categories the fictitious sorters produce will genuinely shape the wireframe; the wrong turns the fictitious tree-testers take will genuinely reshape the labels; the final prototype — grounded in three studies nobody ran — will genuinely be deployed to real teachers.

Two rules keep that honest:

  • Rule 1: invented findings may only encode real beliefs.

    Every finding in these studies encodes something the instructor genuinely believes about real teachers, from years of teaching, observing, and talking with them. The fiction contributes specificity — names, sessions, quotes, numbers. It is never allowed to contribute conviction the instructor doesn’t have.

  • Rule 2: the shipped site must stand without the fiction.

    The teachers who use the redesigned site are owed a site the designer will defend as design judgment, full stop. The studies are the teaching dramatization of that judgment — they are not its evidence, and nothing about the deployed site should ever be justified to a real user by citing them.

The honest limit

Invented data can never surprise its author. Real participants routinely destroy the hypothesis you brought them, and that is the single most valuable thing user research does. These studies are, in the end, the instructor’s design judgment wearing participant costumes. Read them as models of form — how a study is designed, run, reported, and turned into cited decisions — and get your surprises from real users.

What is speculated

One layer of this fiction goes further than invention: it guesses at a future. In March 2026 the First Presidency announced a new Sunday meeting schedule — 25-minute weekly classes, effective September 2026. That change is real. But the companion’s account of the Timpanogos Shadows Ward is set in November 2026, and it was written in August 2026, before the change went live anywhere. Every success, struggle, and adjustment it describes is pure speculation about an experience nobody had yet had. Passages that speculate carry the SPECULATION stamp. Treat them as a designer’s reasoned guess, never as a report.

How invented material is marked

The rule is absolute: invented data is always visibly marked, at the point where it appears. Four layers do this.

  • 1. The banner at the top of every page.

    Injected by companion.js on every companion page, with no way to opt a page out. It is the red bar you scrolled past to get here. (Prototype artifacts carry their own static banner instead, naming them as case-study prototypes.)

  • 2. The FICTITIOUS DATA stamp.

    Attached directly to every invented table, chart, quote block, and number — so that a screenshot, a printout, or a single scrolled section still carries the warning with it. Variants: INVENTED QUOTE on quotations, SPECULATION on guesses about a future that hadn’t happened yet, and CONSTRUCTED on instruments assembled from verified real content.

  • 3. A scope note at the top of every study page.

    Naming the specifics for that page: the deck, the prototype, and the decisions are real; every participant, session, quote, and number between them is invented.

  • 4. This page.

    The canonical statement, linked from the banner on every page of the companion.

If you are ever unsure

Assume any person, session, quote, or number in this companion is invented, and any artifact you can click — the live site, a prototype, a commit — is real. That heuristic is correct everywhere in the chain.
