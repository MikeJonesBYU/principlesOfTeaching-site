A real site, redesigned

This is a course companion. It takes one small site that was really designed, really built, and is really deployed — the Principles of Teaching site — and puts it through Project 1’s design cycle: a card sort, a wireframe, a tree test, a functional prototype, a five-user task test, and a final prototype — six graded turn-ins, one for each. The live site plays the role the project assigns to “an existing version you’re unimpressed by” — and the final prototype will really replace it.

It is written as a designer’s notebook. Each build is tested by the next study; each study’s findings feed the next build. Every study page is a worked example of the study you will run; every prototype is a working artifact you can click through.

One thing you must know first

The site is real. The prototypes are real. The studies are not. Nobody ran these sessions. Every participant, quote, and number in this companion was invented for teaching — and this time the fiction runs forward: the invented findings drive the real redesign. That only works honestly under strict rules, which is why they are written down.

Invented material is always visibly marked — a banner on every page, and a FICTITIOUS DATA stamp on every invented table, chart, quote, and number.

The design cycle, and what you hand in

Three user studies and three prototypes, in one chain: each study informs the build after it, and each build is tested by the study after that. You hand in each one on its own — six turn-ins, alternating study and build, every one of them 60 points until the final prototype. The studies are graded on the Study Report Rubric, the prototypes on the Prototype Rubric; the two rubrics never mix. The diagram below is the Project 1 flow chart, in its own two colors (green for a user study, rust for something you build); the panels under it are the same six turn-ins in order, each one saying exactly what goes in it, what it is graded on, and what it hands forward to the next.

Every station is shown, whether or not its worked example is written yet — unfinished ones say so in place and are not links.
