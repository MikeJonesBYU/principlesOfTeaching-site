CS 356 · Brigham Young University

A real site, studied

This is a course companion. It takes one small site that was really designed, really built, and is really deployed — the Principles of Teaching site — and walks it back through the methods CS 356 teaches: a contextual study, a card sort, the fidelity ladder, a tree test, a five-user task test, and an external review.

It is written as a designer’s notebook. Each study asks a question, runs a method, produces results, and ends with the design decision it changed — cited to the commit where that change actually landed.

One thing you must know first

The site is real. The studies are not. Nobody ran these sessions. Every participant, quote, session, and number in this companion was invented for teaching, and is constructed to be consistent with the real design decisions it illustrates. The site, the decisions, and the git history are real and checkable.

Invented material is always visibly marked — a banner on every page, and a FICTITIOUS DATA stamp on every invented table, chart, quote, and number.
