Protocol. Their own phones, wherever they were. One of us moderated and one took notes. We cleared the build’s Session log before each session, read each task from a card, and then stopped talking; we asked people to say what they were looking for and to read out anything before they tapped it, and to say “I’d give up” whenever they would in real life — that ends the task and counts as a result. Task order was a cyclic Latin square, so every task sat in every position exactly once. Between tasks the participant tapped the title bar to go home, which the log records, so the task boundaries are in the file. We did not answer questions about where things were. Twice we helped someone after a task was marked done — Lyle and Hal both got lost in the Church website tab that “Read it in the manual” opens, and we brought them back; both are in the notes. At the end of each session we tapped Export CSV on the Session log screen and had the file sent from their phone. Sessions ran thirty to fifty-five minutes; Hal’s was the long one.

5. Analysis

FICTITIOUS DATA

  • The trial branch earned its place, and its label worked. Three of five went straight to “What it feels like” for a question phrased in a child’s words, the two who searched landed on the same block at rank one, and the one who went through Teaching about Jesus Christ found it there too. 5 of 5, mean 29.4 seconds, no backtracks — against 3 of 11 with 3 give-ups and 88.8 seconds for the same question in the tree test. The tree test’s open decision closes. What did not work is everything around the label: the tile is sixth of six and below the first screen on a phone (F7), the trial note asks participants a question meant for us, and a branch with one card reads as unfinished to the people it was built for.
  • T5 is a content failure wearing a navigation costume. Four of five first-tapped the right category, four of five reached the right group, and the two give-ups happened on search screens after that. Hal read both accepted cards aloud in the right group and rejected them, because neither card says anything about a child who is not there; Karalee never opened the right group, but the query she quit on had the parent-message card at rank five, and her own words for giving up were that card’s first move. The structure delivered people to the answer; the cards could not tell them it was the answer. That is a writing job for the final prototype, not a filing job.
  • The search fails on function words and on the manual’s verbs. Three of twelve queries put “When you do not know the answer” first or fourth because “no” and “not” prefix-match the word not in that label (F5). “Nothing prepared” returned eight Takes getting ready cards before a usable one, because prepared matches every skill whose sentence starts Prepare (F6). “Absent” matched nothing at all (F1). None of these is the meaning index’s fault; all three are the literal layer, which we can change one line at a time.
  • The prep chip: read by two, used by two, missed by one, and it contradicted a lead once. Both facet uses in the study were the “No prep” chip, both on T3, and both turned a bad list into a good one in one tap. Deanne used the chip on a card to reject a block. Hal never saw a chip at his text size and took the one block on T3 whose lead sounds live and whose chip says otherwise (F4). Of the 22 blocks marked Takes getting ready, we count four whose situation lead reads as an in-the-moment situation (017.title_number2, 023.title_number1, 030.title_number3, 031.title_number2); the chip is derived from the manual’s verb and is right, so it is the leads that have to move.
  • Authored situation leads routed people correctly on the task built to test them. T1 succeeded 5 of 5 and every run ended by choosing the card whose lead we wrote (“not sure the story earns its minutes”), three of them without a search. The card build’s notes worried an authored lead could “confidently route people to the wrong skill”; we saw that once, on 017.title_number2, and it was the chip’s disagreement with the lead that did it, not the lead alone.
  • “You, before Sunday” is ambiguous, and we wrote it. Our tree-test decision D3 added that subtitle so nobody would open the self-preparation group looking for lesson planning. Lyle opened it looking for lesson planning and read the subtitle as confirmation; Hal opened it on a different task and stayed. Two of five, on two tasks, in one group. The subtitle is doing the opposite of its job.
  • The manual button does exactly what the accent was spent on — and then loses people. Four of five tapped it; it is the only accented element on a block page, and nobody hesitated over which thing to tap. Then two of the four, both on Android, could not get back from the new tab (F9). The attention design is right; what happens after the tap is not.
  • The by-moment view is not a door people choose. Two first taps in twenty-five, and the task built for it (T4) drew one. The tree test’s 95.5% success for moment entries was 22 runs, mostly one person, and we said then we would hold it loosely until v1’s logs could carry it. They carried it nowhere: on a phone, people start from the problem or from the search box.
  • Calibration. Hal is two of the three failures. He is one person, and at his text size the layout is a different layout; we count him because seventy-one adults in this ward are over sixty-five, not because five participants make a trend. F10 and F11 are one or two instances each and are listed as what they are. We cannot see scrolling in the log, so everything about long lists is from the notes.

6. Changes we will make

FICTITIOUS DATA

All of these land in the final prototype; the card build is frozen as of this test.

  1. We are keeping the sixth branch, and it stops being a trial. Because T2 succeeded 5 of 5 with 3 of 5 going straight to “What it feels like”; because two people read the trial note aloud as a question to them; and because Deanne asked whether one card was all there was — we will remove the trial note from the interface, keep the label, and cross-list into the branch the blocks that are about what someone feels rather than what the teacher does, starting with 021.title_number1 (help them put what they feel into words), 008.title_number1 (know and feel the love of the Father and the Son) and 007.title_number2 (recognize the Lord’s hand in their own experiences). Directional beyond those three: same rule, applied block by block when the written guidance is drafted.
  2. All six category tiles will fit on one phone screen.
