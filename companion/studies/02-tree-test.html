5. Analysis

FICTITIOUS DATA

  • Our three misses are exactly our three findings. We predicted the modal first click on 7 of 10 tasks (70%) and missed on T01 · Saturday-night prep, T04 · Losing the seven-year-olds and T09 · Is it the Spirit?
  • T09 · Is it the Spirit? is not a labeling problem. The first click scattered three ways with none of them reliably wrong: 6 of 11 opened “Teaching about Jesus Christ,” which contains the answer; 3 opened “Knowing and loving the people you teach” and all three failed; 2 entered by moment.
  • All three give-ups happened on the home screen, not lost in a subtree — each had been inside two or three categories first. The task collected 19 backtracks across 7 runs against 20 for the other nine combined, at 88.8 seconds against a study-wide 32.3.
  • The sort and the test asked different questions. One sorter in eight made a Spirit pile: the sort measured what people group by, the tree test measured what they look for — the exact condition our card-sort report named as reopening the decision.
  • T04 · Losing the seven-year-olds splits by which door you came in. All 4 who entered by lesson moment reached the block in two clicks. Of the 7 who entered by category, 3 reached it, 3 stopped on a wrong block, and 1 gave up after 7 clicks and 96.4 seconds.
  • Both filings of that block are defensible, which is the problem. 2 of the 3 misses ended on “questions about Christ’s symbols,” the adjacent skill on the same manual page, filed under Christ › Finding Him in the scriptures for its symbolism, while the answer — the same page’s other half — sits under Keeping their attention › Something they do, not watch. 5 of 11 first-clicked Christ, found a Christ-symbols skill and reasonably stopped. Our card sort predicted this — a block people reach for from two questions — and we filed it once anyway.
  • T01 · Saturday-night prep passed 10 of 11 and still hid a defect. Of the 9 who first clicked “Getting ready before Sunday,” 5 opened “Getting yourself ready”; the other 4 opened “Planning the lesson” and all four backed out and crossed over — 47.9 seconds against 20.1.
  • Nothing in the results table flags that — a defect priced in seconds, not errors. It is also why we predicted the task wrong: we expected “Saturday night” to read as a moment; it read as a phase of preparation.
  • The failures cluster at the last step. 16 of the 21 non-give-up misses ended inside the correct category, 8 of those inside the correct group: our L1 holds, and what fails is telling sibling leaves or sibling groups apart — a wording problem at L2 and L3. The exception is T09 · Is it the Spirit?, where the failure is at L1.
  • The moment-view aggregate, with its caveat attached. 22 of the 110 runs entered by moment and succeeded 95.5% at a mean of 27.9 seconds; the 88 category-view runs succeeded 72.7% at 33.4 seconds. That looks like a large effect and we are not claiming it is one: Nathan Kim went 10 for 10 entering by moment on all ten of his tasks, Sydnee Hafen on six, and the remaining six runs are spread across three people. 22 self-selected runs against 88 compares participants, not views — a hypothesis for v1’s logs, not a finding.
  • That T04 · Losing the seven-year-olds result is the moment view’s whole case — one task, 4 runs, stated as such, and the reason the attribute survives into v1.

6. Design decisions

FICTITIOUS DATA

Six decisions, all landing in prototype v1; the wireframe is frozen as of this test.

  1. We are reopening the Spirit question. Because T09 · Is it the Spirit? succeeded in only 3 of 11 with 3 give-ups, all three on the home screen; because it collected 19 backtracks against 20 for the other nine tasks combined; because its first click scattered across three branches, none reliably wrong; and because our card-sort report named this condition as reopening the decision — we will build a sixth top-level branch for it into v1 and test it there. The card sort is not overturned (element 5). Its label will come from participants’ own hunting language (“what it feels like”) and is itself what v1 must test.
  2. We are giving the sensory-objects block a second category. Because 5 of 11 first-clicked “Teaching about Jesus Christ” on T04 · Losing the seven-year-olds, 2 of the 3 misses stopped on the adjacent Christ-symbols skill, and all 4 who came in by moment reached the answer in two clicks, we will make category a list rather than a single value and file “a sensory experience with scriptural objects” under both “Keeping their attention › Something they do, not watch” and “Teaching about Jesus Christ › Finding Him in the scriptures.” We will also relabel the leaf to lead with the doing, not the symbolism.
  3. We are relabeling the two preparation groups. Because all 4 who opened “Planning the lesson” first on T01 · Saturday-night prep crossed over to “Getting yourself ready,” at 47.9 seconds against 20.1 for the 5 who went straight there, we will rename them: “Getting yourself ready” gains the subtitle “you, before Sunday,” and “Planning the lesson” becomes “Planning what happens in class.” The pair costs no failures and about 28 seconds a head.
  4. We are rewriting the leaf labels inside “Asking so you get a real answer.” Because 2 of the 3 runs lost on T03 · A question you can’t answer stopped on “create open-ended search questions,” and because 16 of the 21 misses ended inside the correct category with 8 of them inside the correct group, v1’s leaf labels will name the situation the skill is for, not restate the manual sentence.
  5. We are keeping the five categories. Because 9 of 10 tasks had the intended category as their most common first click, because 85 of 110 runs ended on the correct block, and because 16 of the 21 misses stayed inside the correct category, the L1 is not what is breaking and we will not reorganize it. The Spirit branch in decision 1 adds to this structure rather than replacing it.
  6. We are keeping the lesson-moment attribute. Because all 4 who entered T04 · Losing the seven-year-olds by moment reached the correct block in two clicks while the category view split five ways, v1 keeps moment on every block and keeps the by-moment view — not on the aggregate 95.5% (element 8).

Both teams put the same situation in front of a participant. Report A’s T09 and Report B’s B10 are almost the same sentence: a woman in your class says she can never tell whether a feeling is the Spirit or just her own thoughts — find something that would help her. It is the hardest thing either structure was asked to do, and neither structure did it. That is where the two reports stop resembling each other.

Team A measured it. 3 of 11 found the block, 3 gave up, average 88.8 seconds, 19 backtracks in 7 runs, all three abandonments happening on the home screen after the participant had already been inside two or three categories. Then they diagnosed it: the first click scattered three ways with none of them reliably wrong, which is the signature of a missing branch rather than a bad label. Then they spent it. Their card-sort report had named the exact condition that would reopen the Spirit question; the tree test met the condition; the decision reopens, and prototype v1 gets a sixth branch to test. A finding travelled from one study through a prototype into another study and out the far side as a design commitment.

Team B keyed it to C15, marked all six participants wrong, and wrote that the task was probably worded badly. They were not careless about it — they noticed the pattern in the data (three people volunteered that C14 was “the closest thing”), they reported it honestly, and they took the blame themselves. The failure is upstream of effort. Nobody on the team ever asked whether the answer key was right, so six participants’ worth of evidence about a real gap in their structure was filed under “bad task” and thrown away. The same thing happened to B07, where the keyed answer is a block their wireframe does not contain: the study measured the key, and the team read the measurement as a fact about the world.

Same moment, opposite epistemics. That is most of the 29 points, and almost none of it is effort or polish.
